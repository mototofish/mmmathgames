    📚 給家長的話 · For Parents

    🎯 學習重點 · Learning Focus

    邏輯推理題是幼小銜接的經典題型，看似沒有「計數」，其實是數學思維的核心：孩子要從文字線索中抽取條件，一步步收窄可能性。本遊戲涵蓋四種基本推理方法 — 「排除法」（把不可能的逐個排除，剩下的就是答案）、「排序」（由「贏了幾多」推出名次，即由數量到順序）、「一一對應配對」（三種顏色配三個圖案，用「是」和「不是」逐步確定），以及「遞移比較」（A 比 B 重、B 比 C 重，所以 A 比 C 重 — 這是日後學習不等式和數線的基礎）。這些能力同時是閱讀理解和小一面試的重要根基。

    Logic puzzles are a classic of early-years math: children must extract conditions from verbal clues and narrow down the possibilities step by step. This game covers four core reasoning skills — elimination (rule out the impossible; what remains must be true), ordering (turning "beat two animals" into a finishing place, i.e. quantity into position), one-to-one matching (three colours, three patterns — pinning them down with "is" and "is not"), and transitive comparison (A is heavier than B, B is heavier than C, so A is heavier than C — the seed of inequalities and number lines). These are also the foundations of reading comprehension and primary-school interview tasks.

    👨‍👩‍👧 陪玩小貼士 · Playing Together

    孩子答錯時，不要急著公布答案 — 把線索逐條再讀一次，每讀一條問：「這條線索告訴我們什麼？哪個選項一定不可能？」鼓勵孩子用手指把排除掉的選項「劃走」，並說出理由：「這個不可能，因為……」說得出理由，才是真的推理。排名題可以借玩具公仔實物排一排；配對題可以畫簡單的表格（顏色一行、圖案一列），在格仔內打勾打叉 — 這就是日後「邏輯方陣」的雛形。

    When your child gets stuck, don't rush to reveal the answer — re-read the clues one by one and ask: "What does this clue tell us? Which option is impossible now?" Encourage them to "cross out" eliminated options with a finger and say the reason out loud: "This one can't be right, because..." Being able to SAY the reason is real reasoning. For ranking questions, line up toy figures for real; for matching questions, draw a simple grid (colours down the side, patterns across the top) and tick or cross the cells — that's the seed of the classic logic grid.

    🏠 生活延伸練習 · Everyday Practice

    生活中處處是邏輯題：吃飯時問「爸爸第一個吃完，我比媽媽快，誰最慢？」；出門時玩「猜猜看」— 「我們不去公園，也不去沙灘，你猜我們去哪？」；收拾襪子時玩配對 — 「這對是爸爸的，這對不是紅色，那是什麼顏色？」乘升降機時比一比身高 — 「我比弟弟高，弟弟比表妹高，誰最矮？」每天一句小線索，孩子的推理力就會慢慢長出來。

    Logic is everywhere in daily life: at dinner, ask "Dad finished first, I was faster than Mum — who was slowest?"; on the way out, play "guess where" — "We're not going to the park, and not to the beach — where are we going?"; matching socks: "This pair is Dad's, this pair is NOT red — what colour is it?"; in the lift, compare heights: "I'm taller than my brother, he's taller than our cousin — who is shortest?" One little clue a day, and reasoning grows on its own.
